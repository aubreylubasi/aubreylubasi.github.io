The problem

One expired paper can keep a crew at the gate

Documents expire quietly

Medicals, inductions, training certificates, tax clearance and NAPSA all run out on different dates. Nobody notices until a worker is turned away.

The file is everywhere

When a mine asks for the contractor file, it is spread across paper folders, WhatsApp chats and old spreadsheets. Putting it together takes days.

Mines are asking for more

Zambia's local content rules (SI 68 of 2025) bring more local firms into mine supply chains. Every one of them must meet the same site requirements.

How it works

Three steps to site-ready

1

Load your documents

Add your company papers and each worker's certificates, with issue and expiry dates. Import your worker list straight from Excel.

2

Get warned early

MineReady warns you 30 days before anything expires (you can change this), and sends a weekly summary to the owner. Each worker shows green, amber or red for every site.

3

Send the mine a complete file

One click builds a PDF contractor file and an Excel register. Or send the mine's coordinator a secure, read-only link instead of attachments.

What's inside

Made for how contractors really work

Company file

PACRA, ZRA tax clearance, NAPSA, Workers' Compensation, insurance and safety policies, each with its expiry date.

Worker register

Every worker's medical, induction, training and licences in one place, checked against what each site requires.

Expiry alerts

Email and SMS reminders before anything lapses, so renewals happen before the gate, not at it.

Supervisor phone app

Check any worker's status at the gate, even with no signal, and photograph renewed certificates on site.

One-click contractor pack

A PDF file with an index, plus an Excel register, ready for the mine's contractor coordinator.

Secure share links

Read-only links that expire, can need a PIN, can be cancelled at any time, and record every view.
